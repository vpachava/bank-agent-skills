DWH extracts the minimum card/customer fields required by the UiPath RPA automation platform and synchronizes them with the RPA database.

4. Offer submission to Digital Engagement

RPA calls a Digital Engagement REST API.
Eligible records are sent in batches of 500.
RPA continues submitting batches until all eligible records have been processed.
Digital Engagement places accepted records onto Amazon MQ and returns HTTP 200 to RPA.
For non-success responses, RPA retries.
The Digital Engagement API uses batch ID/correlation ID/idempotency controls to prevent duplicate records from being placed into the queue.
5. Engagement processing and customer notification

Digital Engagement consumes records from the queue.
It retrieves customer contact details.
It persists relevant engagement/offer information in PostgreSQL on Amazon RDS.
It publishes an event to Event Hub for customer notification.
Based on notification type/event, the notification adapter retrieves the appropriate communication template.
It populates placeholders such as proposed limit and last four card digits.
It calls the notification service to deliver push notification, SMS, and/or email.
Notifications contain a deep link incorporating the RPA reference ID.
6. Customer acceptance

A customer can:

Click the deep link in the notification, or

The mobile application calls the backend to verify:

Deep-link validity

Card details
Offer validity/expiry

Any other required eligibility/control conditions

Current credit limit
Proposed credit limit
The customer provides consent/acceptance.

Digital Engagement stores the consent and calls an RPA API to notify RPA of the customer's acceptance.

Digital Engagement must obtain an Azure AD token to securely invoke the RPA API.

7. Final credit decision

RPA calls the Credit Engine to obtain bureau-related fields required for final credit-policy evaluation.
Credit Engine checks whether an AECB credit report is available for the customer from the previous 30 days.
If no valid recent report exists, Credit Engine retrieves a fresh AECB report.
Credit Engine extracts the required bureau fields.
The AECB report is uploaded to the bank's Document Management System (DMS).
Credit Engine sends the required credit-summary fields back to RPA through an RPA callback API.
Credit Engine obtains an Azure AD token before invoking the RPA endpoint.
RPA applies the final credit rules and determines whether the customer remains eligible for the proposed increase.
8. Card-limit update

If approved, RPA calls a middleware REST API.
Middleware invokes the Fiserv card-limit-update REST API.
The resulting response is placed onto MQ.
RPA polls/consumes the response from MQ.
RPA updates its internal database with the final status.
9. Customer notification and cooling-off controls

RPA publishes the final success/failure event to Event Hub.
Event Hub drives customer communication through SMS, email, and/or push notification.
The customer is informed whether the credit-limit increase succeeded or failed.
Following either success or failure, the customer should not be considered for another CLI offer for a configurable period of X days.
On the next mobile-app login, the customer's updated credit limit should be immediately visible.
